# Extension-of-Stay Tracking After the End of Duration-of-Status

**One line:** DHS replaces open-ended "duration of status" admission for F, J and I nonimmigrants
with a fixed admission period, forcing recurring Form I-539 extension filings from 2026-09-15.

## Job to be done
Every affected nonimmigrant must now track an expiry date and file extensions of stay, where
previously status ran open-ended. Commenters in the rule warned of "the sheer scale of new I-539
submissions contemplated by this rule" and "a dramatic increase of students losing their status."
Source: [DHS 2026-14439](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant).

## Forcing function
Final rule, effective **2026-09-15**. Consequence of missing it is loss of lawful status —
about as strong a B1 as exists.

## Buyer — and the structural twist
Population is large: ~301,000 J-1 entrants in 2024 per SEVP's SEVIS by the Numbers, plus the
much larger F-1 population.

The interesting part is *who* carries the burden. A commenter noted the rule "assumes that DSOs
and AROs will provide students and exchange visitors with Form I-539 application support, but
that many schools do not allow their officials to undertake this work, as it could constitute
unauthorized practice of law."

So the obligation does **not** land on the university — the party Terra Dotta, Sunapsis, ISSM and
Ellucian already sell to. It lands on the individual, who has no vendor. That is exactly the
"obligated party existing vendors do not sell to" pattern the rubric now looks for.

## Why it dies anyway

**G7 — custody/licensed-activity trap.** The reason schools won't touch this is the reason a solo
founder can't either: advising on an I-539 is unauthorized practice of law in most states. A
product that guides a filer through the form is doing the thing the universities' own counsel
forbids. Fixing that means partnering with licensed attorneys — a legal ramp far past 90 days.

**G3 — zero-shot-able.** Stripped of legal advice, what remains is deadline tracking and form
prefill. A chatbot plus a calendar reminder covers it.

**Adjacency.** Boundless, SimpleCitizen and Lawfully already occupy consumer immigration filing
with real funding.

## What this case teaches
The rubric's "obligated party has no vendor" test found something real here — but *why* they have
no vendor mattered more than *that* they have none. **An unserved buyer is sometimes unserved for
a legal reason, not an oversight.** Add that question to the G8 check: when a population is
obviously burdened and obviously unserved, look for the licensing or liability barrier first.
